### V1 details that change the design

- **Universe and dates:** §2.1 and Appendix A, printed pp98 and 113: nine established equity factors plus 94 anomaly portfolios; NYSE/Amex/Nasdaq ordinary common stock data, July 1926–December 2016, with different start dates by strategy. Daily and monthly CRSP, Compustat and IBES inputs. The anomaly construction excludes financials, prices below $5 and market capitalizations below the first NYSE decile. Delisting returns replace missing returns where supplied by CRSP. These filters make transfer to newly launched tokens especially uncertain.
- **Construction:** §2.2, Eqs (3)–(4), p98: monthly excess return is scaled by the inverse of the previous month’s realized daily-return variance. The constant is selected to match full-sample unconditional variance. The authors explicitly note this constant is not known in real time, while unconstrained Sharpe ratios are invariant to a fixed positive scale. That is a narrower point than saying that every historical Sharpe comparison is invalid.
- **In-sample versus investable:** §4.1, pp101–103: a positive spanning-regression intercept can coexist with a lower Sharpe ratio for the managed portfolio alone. Ex-post optimal combination weights are not known at the decision time. Avoid optimizing council weights on the final test data.
- **Forward-like construction:** §4.3.1, pp106–107: initial training is 120 months, then expanding-window estimation of the scale, mean returns and covariance; baseline risk aversion is five, absolute leverage cap five. These are their study choices, not KESTREL defaults. Table 7 varies training lengths, rolling versus expanding windows, risk aversion and leverage constraints.
- **Balanced interpretation:** some strategies, including momentum-related factors, do benefit in their sample. This is counterevidence to universal volatility-management claims, not proof that volatility controls never help. Their return and CER statistics do not establish that Solana fees, AMM impact, MEV or failed exits are affordable. No explicit memecoin execution-cost model is present in the sections read. “Zero-cost portfolio” in this paper means a net-investment long–short portfolio; it does **not** mean costless execution.

### V2 details that change the design

- §3, p9 footnote 2 and pp10–11 distinguish the August 1926 data start from the common August 1936 evaluation start. Do not report all dates as one uninterrupted out-of-sample test.
- §2.1 states that a fixed positive scale does not change unconstrained Sharpe; changing the scale changes leverage, drawdowns and what constraints bind. Rolling scale estimation can also change the return series. Therefore, match risk and constraints in KESTREL comparisons rather than scaling a successful chart after observing the result.
- §3, p9 uses a leverage cap of two in constrained variants; the working paper compares HAC Sharpe inference and Newey–West alpha inference. These historical significance calculations are not directly reusable for dependent, sparse token outcomes.
- The claimed severe drawdowns concern the paper’s leveraged equity-market strategies. Do not quote those percentages as forecasts for KESTREL. Do not interpret an insignificant result as proof that the economic effect is exactly zero.

## Proposed validation protocol for the two-advisor council

1. **Freeze the estimand before examining outcomes.** Primary target: incremental net account return of the council policy minus the unchanged primary policy, using identical eligible opportunities, clock, risk budget, executable quotes and cost rules. Also report mean net trade return, tail loss, drawdown, turnover, capacity and rejection quality. Filtering value, ranking value and sizing value are separate experiments.
2. **Persist the information boundary.** Retain provider event time, request start, response receive time, parser completion and advisor decision time where available. Feature availability is the latest actual receive/compute time of its inputs. Replayed decisions can consume only records already available then. Historical provider responses fetched today cannot establish what an old decision could have known.
3. **Use chronological blocks.** Define rolling training and validation periods only after observing dataset density, without optimizing their lengths for return. Use a sealed terminal holdout collected after specification freeze. Do not inspect this holdout through dashboard summaries before the one registered final evaluation. If inspected, it becomes development data; create a new forward holdout.
4. **Purge by interval overlap.** Each labeled opportunity has an interval from decision to its latest required outcome/exit observation. Drop training labels overlapping any evaluation interval. Embargo at least the maximum evaluation label horizon plus provider availability delay around adjacent boundaries. Record the exact interval rule; do not substitute arbitrary random folds. Historical lookbacks may overlap legitimately, but labels or later-refitted transforms must never leak into training.
5. **Count dependent evidence honestly.** Multiple pools for the same mint, overlapping entries, shared market shocks and repeated minute polls are not independent trials. Assign token identity and decision-day/market-time clusters; compare policies using paired daily net returns on a common calendar. Use a moving or stationary block bootstrap with all policies/assets resampled jointly, and preregister plausible block lengths from training-only dependence diagnostics. Report sensitivity to multiple block lengths and explicitly mark short histories with too few blocks as insufficient.
6. **Register the entire search family.** Before evaluation, log hypotheses, advisor versions, parameter sets, permitted universes, metrics, cost variants, stop rules and all failed attempts. For a small confirmatory family, use Holm step-down family-wise error control at 5% across the registered advisor/policy claims, with valid dependence-aware per-claim p-values. Do not present the best of many runs as one test. Exploratory parameter sweeps are exploratory; they cannot promote influence by themselves.
7. **Predeclare the economic hurdle.** For the $100 replica and the learning book separately, quantify minimum worthwhile incremental gain after realistic costs and an error reserve. Set the practical hurdle before outcomes are viewed. Promotion requires the multiplicity-adjusted lower confidence bound to exceed that hurdle and risk constraints to remain satisfied. If uncertainty is wide, collect more independent time blocks; a fixed count of trades cannot waive this requirement. Check only at registered evaluation dates or use an explicitly justified sequential method; repeatedly checking ordinary 95% intervals invalidates their advertised error rate.
8. **Run matched ablations.** Primary alone; primary+A; primary+B; primary+A+B; NO TRADE; simple signal and simple risk-control baselines. Initial advisory outputs have zero trading influence. Counterfactual filters initially omit the same rejected trades without silently recycling their freed capital; later portfolio simulation can test recycling as a distinct registered policy. Hold initial capital, maximum exposure, opportunity feed and capital accounting constant.
9. **Treat missing exits as outcomes.** Retain dead tokens, vanished pools, rejected transactions and price/quote gaps. Never remove a losing asset because its current endpoint disappeared. If no executable liquidation exists, record unresolved exposure and valuation bounds, including a zero-recovery scenario. Do not mark a stale chart close as realizable cash. Trading resumes only with real fresh inputs after sleep/network gaps.
10. **Stress actual execution assumptions.** Base case includes quote spread/AMM impact already embedded in executable quotes plus additional observed or conservatively assumed transaction costs, network priority fees, potential adverse movement between quote and fill and failed-attempt costs. Avoid charging the same spread/impact twice. Stress quote delay, worse adverse selection, failed attempts, disappearing exit routes and pool changes. Plot cost break-even surfaces by position size and liquidity. No model can guarantee a stop fills at its requested price.
11. **Separate evidence types.** Synthetic fixtures prove arithmetic and safety behavior; historical replay estimates past policy behavior subject to archival limitations; fresh SHADOW predicts later outcomes with frozen inputs; PAPER tests accounting and simulated execution; only a genuine future observation supplies forward evidence. A $100,000 book cannot validate the capacity or survivability of a real $100 account.
12. **Promotion is staged and reversible.** No influence → qualified PAPER filtering trial → separately qualified ranking trial → separately qualified sizing trial. Require an untouched-period result, acceptable downside and cost-stress survival for each role. Drift, broken inputs, unexpected advisor error correlation, risk breach or loss of executable routes demotes the relevant advisor immediately to zero influence. Later positive evidence cannot bypass security, STOP, guardian or LIVE authorization.

### Practical interpretation of disagreement

Independent economic stories do not prove independent forecasts. Report conditional return/loss distributions for A/B agreement, conflict and abstention; their correlations with the primary score; and dependence conditional on liquidity, asset age and market regime. A signal that adds no incremental information after these shared dependencies is redundant even if its standalone chart looks attractive. Estimate correlations only over matched observations, retain sample size and uncertainty, and do not convert SUPPORT/OPPOSE votes to an uncalibrated success probability.

### First useful acceptance criteria

- Every recorded advisor decision is reproducible from immutable, point-in-time input identifiers and a versioned specification.
- No changes to current collector cadence, strategy decisions, PAPER sizing, signing policy or LIVE gates occur during this research/design pass.
- At least one fixture demonstrates that a future observation changes a later decision but cannot change an earlier replayed decision; one demonstrates that a vanished pool remains a loss/unresolved exposure; one demonstrates exclusion of overlapping labels.
- A report can compute all four ablations on identical opportunities without netting learning-book profit against the $100 replica or counting observations as independent trades.
- The report can state **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE** without fallback to synthetic success, calendar time alone, a raw win rate, or a nominal trade count.
