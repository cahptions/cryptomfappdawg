# Cryptocurrency evidence and transfer limits

Research date: 2026-10-19. This is a research document, not a trading change. No collector, provider credentials, signer, wallet, or trading configuration was touched.

## Conclusion for the council design

There is enough verified primary material to justify **testing** cost-qualified persistence and reversion hypotheses. These documents do **not** establish either edge for newly issued Solana memecoins. Keep both advisors observational. In particular, a profitable historical long/short derivatives backtest does not validate a spot-only “buy a dip” adaptation, and a protocol whitepaper establishes execution mechanics rather than alpha.

A second advisor based on temporary liquidity dislocation/recovery must be labelled experimental. Structural deterioration, supply changes, or an exit route disappearing can look like a statistical overshoot. Historical chart reversion without a contemporaneous executable exit is not a successful trade.

Full source metadata, exact Git commits, SHA-256 hashes, blocked access, and rejected mislabeled PDFs are in [sources/crypto_manifest.json](sources/crypto_manifest.json). Downloaded third-party papers were kept in `/tmp/council-sources`, not vendored into the application.

## What could not be verified

NBER full-text endpoints for Liu–Tsyvinski *Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency* and Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu *Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency* returned explicit proxy CONNECT 403 responses. Initial arXiv and author-site probes were also denied. No proxy bypass, relay, credential read, or provider-market request was used.

Makarov–Schoar *Trading and Arbitrage in Cryptocurrency Markets* and Cong–Li–Tang–Yang *Crypto Wash Trading* remain useful research leads, but no verified primary copy was obtained. This pass makes no numerical or empirical claims from those unread papers. The manifest labels them `not_read`, not “abstract read.” Publication metadata for blocked leads is provisional.

One library PDF named as a Bitcoin-volatility/whale paper was actually *Online Control of Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search Using Deep Reinforcement Learning*, by Reijnen et al. It was rejected after reading the title page. Filenames alone are not evidence.

## Transfer from equities and liquid crypto to memecoins

1. **Sessions and discontinuities.** Crypto trades continuously, but a laptop does not observe continuously. Record observation gaps as gaps. The absence of an equity close does not justify forward-filled crypto bars or treating a network outage as zero volatility. For equities, preserve session/auction/corporate-action metadata; for crypto, preserve UTC timestamps and token/pool migration boundaries.
2. **Economic relation versus shared risk.** C1 estimates a statistical long/short relation among established derivatives. Two tokens both being “crypto” does not create an economic price anchor. A token’s SOL beta can change abruptly. A residual is an empirical forecast input, not fair value.
3. **Long-only adaptation.** KESTREL cannot reproduce a market-neutral pair without the short leg, borrow, funding, and margin infrastructure. A spot-only dip/recovery advisor is a new directional hypothesis and must be evaluated on its own.
4. **Fragmentation and pool changes.** C2 §2.1 and C6 pool definitions show that venue/pool identity and paths matter. A new pool for the same mint is not automatically the same liquidity or executable history. Do not splice across migrations unless a verified bridge rule is registered; otherwise reset warm-up.
5. **Volume and ownership.** The OHLCV field documents reported trading, not independent demand. No wash-trading-rate estimate was verified here. Do not equate buys-minus-sells counts with true order-flow imbalance, or sum overlapping rolling one-hour volume snapshots into invented bar volume. Holder concentration, mint/freeze permissions, and liquidity withdrawal remain primary security concerns.
6. **Supply and liquidity withdrawals.** A falling reserve-ratio price after a token supply change, removed liquidity, or stale oracle is not proof of temporary overreaction. C6 permits withdrawal and administration. Preserve the security pipeline and require fresh executable observations before assigning SUPPORT.
7. **Trading costs.** C1’s maker rebates are unavailable to a Jupiter taker strategy by default. C2’s fee-adjusted invariant demonstrates nonlinearity with size. Use quote-size-specific entry and exit observations in the actual quote currency; do not assume zero costs, a constant spread, or a fill at a candle close.
8. **MEV and transaction ordering.** C2 §2.2 explicitly discusses atomic manipulation and miner ordering in its Ethereum setting. This does not measure Solana MEV. Include delayed/worse fills and failed transaction cost stress scenarios; regard a precise Solana MEV cost model as unknown until execution telemetry exists.

## Execution accounting requirements for the proposed advisors

Use this only as a sanity check for a simple constant-product pool: with token reserves `x`, quote reserves `y`, quote input `q`, and input fee fraction `f`, output is `b = x(1-f)q / (y + (1-f)q)`. It follows algebraically from C2’s invariant, under those assumptions. It is not a substitute for Jupiter quotes or support for concentrated-liquidity, custom-token, virtual-reserve, or multi-hop routes.

Every evaluated shadow opportunity needs the requested quote input, executable token output, quote timestamp/receipt timestamp, route/pool identity, fee inclusion metadata, estimated network/priority fees, and a later executable exit output for that token amount. Missing exit quote is unresolved or blocked, not a zero-cost sale. Fees already embedded in route output must not be deducted twice; record separately which fees are included. Likewise, do not double-count a generic impact penalty on top of the same modeled pool impact. Candle high/low does not prove either a stop or target was fillable, or which occurred first.

Treat a same-time buy-quote/sell-quote round trip as a current *cost diagnostic*. It is not evidence of the future exit price, landed execution, guaranteed route availability, or an actual round trip after the trade itself changes reserves. Estimate future profitability from separately recorded future executable observations, with explicitly stated fill assumptions.

## Consequences for the two strategy specifications

- A **market-relative persistence** advisor is more distinct from token breakouts if it measures whether a token's return persists after removing broad-market movement, rather than adding another EMA crossover. But a lookback, residual definition, or parameter choice is a new hypothesis; no verified source here validates 5-minute memecoin residual momentum.
- A **liquidity-qualified dislocation/recovery** advisor should require stabilization/recovery rather than support every negative Z-score. Temporary imbalance and structural loss are competing explanations. C1 motivates studying reversion but does not validate this adaptation. Its baseline is a simple, delayed price-recovery test with identical costs and risk.
- Neither advisor should be promoted because a historical paper reports a high Sharpe ratio. The relevant test is incremental net performance of the existing KESTREL primary engine, in matched-risk ablations and genuine forward observations.
- For both, start with existing locally collected data and no extra provider calls. A clearly labelled ABSTAIN due to unavailable history is useful output. Add bounded optional data acquisition only after a registered information-gain question and an endpoint/terms/budget check.
